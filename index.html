Piano practice that listens like a teacher.

Fresh exercises for sight reading, ear training, rhythm, scales and chords, played on your own digital piano. Every note comes in over MIDI and comes back with the things a teacher would point out: the thumb that arrives late, the off-beat you rush, the hand that leaps between chords.

Free, no account. Works in Chrome and Edge with any USB or Bluetooth MIDI keyboard, and as an app for iPad and Mac.

It hears what a teacher hears

Right notes are only the start. After every run you get a short, specific account of how you played it, in milliseconds and in plain words.

Timing
Every note against the beat, with your Bluetooth and speaker delay measured and taken out first.
Evenness of touch
How much your key velocity varies across a scale, and whether the thumb lands heavier than the other fingers.
Hand changes
Timing bumps that recur at the same place in every octave, named by finger and crossing: thumb under, or finger over.
Two hands at once
How far apart your hands land when they should play together, and each hand’s timing on its own.
Rushing and dragging
Your average lean ahead of or behind the beat, per rhythm figure, so you know it is the off-beat eighth you rush.
Keeping the pulse
The metronome falls silent for whole bars; the app measures how far you drift without it.
Finding chords
How long you take to find each chord, and which chord types, roots and inversions keep you searching.
Chord changes
Whether the keys of a chord land together, and how far your hand travels between chords compared with the smoothest way.

Five ways to practise, one skill at a time

Sight reading

A new passage every time, written for your level: from a single middle C up to both hands in keys with six sharps or flats, with chords and syncopation. Play it at your own pace with the cursor waiting for you, or in time with a metronome and a moving playhead.

  • One difficulty slider from 1 to 100, with key, meter, rhythm and tempo there to override.
  • Notes you keep missing or reach slowly come back more often in weak-spot practice.
  • Clean engraving with note names, chords and both hands lined up beat for beat.

Ear training

Listen to a short phrase and play it back. Notes appear on the staff as you find them, so you learn to hear and to read at the same time. It starts with two neighbouring notes and grows into melodies in new keys, harmonic intervals and chords.

  • Replay as often as you need; a hint shows the next note.
  • A cadence sets the key before phrases that give you no starting note.
  • Intervals you miss most are featured until they stick.

Rhythm

Rhythm without the notes in the way. Each hand taps its own key, so the app can tell your hands apart: one keeps the beat while the other syncopates, or both fill each other’s gaps, up to four against three.

  • Read a rhythm and play it with the metronome.
  • Echo a bar you have just heard; the notation appears after you answer.
  • Pulse keeps going through silent bars and shows how far you drift.

Scales and arpeggios

Majors, all three minors, chromatic, pentatonic and blues scales, plus major, minor, dominant and diminished seventh arpeggios, in all twelve keys. Hands alone, together or in contrary motion, with standard fingering printed over the notes.

  • Each scale has its own tempo ladder: four bpm faster after every clean run.
  • Scales you have played come back for review on a spaced schedule, sooner when they were shaky.
  • Hide the music and play from memory when you are ready.

Chords and progressions

Find triads, sus chords and sevenths on every root, from a chord symbol or from the staff, in root position or a given inversion. Then play the progressions songs are made of, from I–V–vi–IV to the 12-bar blues, in all twelve keys, with the left hand on the root if you like.

  • Any octave and any voicing counts; the app listens for the notes you hold, and for the bass when it matters.
  • Symbols and Roman numerals over the smoothest voicings, or a bare chord chart to voice yourself.
  • The same tempo ladder and spaced review as scales; the chords you find slowly come back more often.

Made for a real piano

Measures your setup

Tap along with a click for a few seconds and the app measures how late your notes arrive and how late you hear the metronome, including Bluetooth headphones on the piano. Timing is judged after that delay is removed.

Pairs over Bluetooth

USB and Bluetooth MIDI in Chrome and Edge. The iPad and Mac app pairs a Bluetooth piano from inside the app. No MIDI at hand? The on-screen keys and your computer keyboard work too.

Hands stay on the keys

After an exercise, play C to try again, D for the next one, F or G for one level easier or harder. A tap on the sustain pedal starts the metronome.

Progress you can trust

Levels only count when the notes are right. Mastering a band takes several different passages at 90 % or better, and experience points grow with accuracy, not with how far you turn the slider up.

Every lesson keeps its own weak spots: notes per clef, intervals, rhythm figures with the milliseconds you rush them by, chord types you find slowly, and scales and progressions by tempo and review date. One click turns them into the next exercise.

How it teaches

One skill at a time. Reading, hearing, rhythm, technique and harmony are practised separately, so you always know what you are working on.

Accuracy before speed. Tempo and level go up only on top of clean playing.

Always new material. Reading and ear exercises are generated fresh. Scales, chords and progressions are a fixed repertoire, kept fresh by shuffled keys and spaced review.

Only what a piano can tell. Fingering is shown, never graded; everything that is graded comes from the notes you play.

The full teaching concept is written down in the project.
